**Round 9 change: added one new voter, `pullback`.**

What I saw in the data: every one of the worst 10 trades had trend, volatility and market_regime all at +1 — the committee was maximally bullish and still wrong. So no existing voter was "wrong"; they were all measuring the same thing (is this an uptrend?) and none of them measured *where in the move you're buying*.

I hand-checked two trades against the raw bars:

| trade | close vs its 20-day average | result |
|---|---|---|
| INTC entry 2025-10-29 | ~2.2 ATR above | −$258, stopped in 4 bars |
| INTC entry 2025-10-03 | ~3.5 ATR above | −$262, stopped in 8 bars |
| AVGO entry 2025-09-04 (winner) | ~0.1 ATR above | +$799, hit target |

In plain terms: the stop sits 1 ATR below entry. If you buy after a stock has already sprinted 2–3 ATRs away from its own average, the first normal breather takes it straight through your stop. Buying near the average leaves room.

The new voter measures exactly that one thing — `(close − 20-day average) ÷ ATR` — and votes 0 at the average, −1 at 3 ATRs above, linear in between (weight 1.0, deliberately un-saturated so it ranks rather than lumps, the lesson from round 8).

Why this should survive the acceptance rule rather than just shrink the trade count: 148 of the 177 trades score 4.5+, so a penalty of at most 1.0 rarely drops one under the 3.5 gate, and 865 signals are already being discarded for lack of slots — so knocked-out entries get backfilled by less-extended candidates. It mostly reranks.
