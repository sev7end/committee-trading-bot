Round 6 change is in place. I raised the market-regime voter's weight from 1.0 to 2.0 and the entry minimum score from 2.5 to 3.5 in `config/params.json`, and wrote the hypothesis to `results/rounds/round_6.hypothesis.txt`.

**What the change does.** With weight 2.0, a bearish SPY vote drags the maximum possible total down to 2.3, below the new 3.5 bar, so no stock can be bought while the market is below its 50-day average. On normal days every total rises by exactly 1.0, so raising the minimum score by the same amount keeps the entry set and the fill ranking identical. The only trades removed are the ones taken against the regime.

**Why it should help.** The best run's max drawdown is the Sep 12 to Dec 17, 2025 slide. Of the 7 trades taken while the regime vote was bearish, 5 lost and 2 won, for a net of roughly -$115, and one loser (GOOGL, late November) sits inside that drawdown window. From the trade log I estimate P&L rises to about $9,900 and drawdown falls to about $2,400, giving a recovery ratio near 4.1 against the 3.83 required. Slots freed at regime flips, such as April 9, may admit an extra trade, which is unquantified.

**What I could not do.** No shell was available in this session or to a subagent, so I could not run the backtest. The estimates come from hand analysis of the best run's trade and equity logs, not from a test run. A prepared sweep script sits in the scratchpad at `sweep.py` if someone with a shell wants to check 38 one-change variants at once.

**Ideas I ruled out from the log.** Halving risk per trade to equalize dollar risk across volatile and calm names would cut P&L by roughly a third, since the high-volatility tech names produced the April-May gains. Requiring 4 agreeing voters removes a set of trades that netted about +$250. Trades where the trend voter was neutral netted -$266 overall but no available knob isolates them cleanly. Cutting breakout weight, the extension voter, and wider stops were already tried and reverted.
